Value proposition

A grid-positive building supplies more and demands less than a business-as-usual building.

Today a new building is a load. It joins the connection queue, waits, and sometimes does not get built at all. A grid-positive building is designed from its first sketch to contribute to the grid. It carries storage, balances itself internally, and offers capacity back to the network.

For a developer, that turns the hardest constraint in the project into a feature of the proposal.

Problem

Demand is rising faster than the grid can grow.

Electrification of heat, transport and industry is pushing demand up. Grid expansion takes years, and the queue for new connections keeps growing. In regions like Utrecht, new housing is already being held back by the lack of transport capacity.

Every workaround so far, such as allocating scarce capacity or relaxing permits, adds pressure to the same network. If this is not solved, electricity becomes the limit on construction and, behind it, on economic growth.

Solution

The building becomes a piece of the grid.

We design the building as a small grid of its own, one with negative load capacity. Instead of asking the network for capacity, it gives some back.

01 · STORAGE

A battery inside the building

A building-scale battery energy storage system (BESS) is part of the design from day one, not a bolt-on.

02 · SUPPLY

Charged with curtailed wind energy

The battery absorbs energy from wind farms that would otherwise be switched off, at the moments the grid has too much.

03 · NEIGHBOURHOOD

Behind-the-meter, shared with neighbours

Surrounding buildings connect behind the meter, so energy is exchanged locally and the main grid is spared.

04 · PRIORITY

Built for the ACM priority framework

The design answers category 1 (congestion relievers) and category 3 (basic needs, which includes housing) of the ACM prioritisation framework at the same time.

05 · MARKETS

Ready for the markets that are coming

Energy trading between buildings, and between a building's components, is on its way. A grid-positive building is designed to take part in it from the first day.

Market

Many policies are moving in the same direction.

New housing needs grid capacity, and policy in the Netherlands, the EU and beyond is shifting toward buildings that are flexible and grid-friendly. The timeline shows how these initiatives line up.

Mar 2025

NLCourt annuls ACM prioritisation framework

The Trade and Industry Appeals Tribunal (CBb) finds the first framework insufficiently reasoned and asks the ACM to rework it. Source

Dec 2025

EUEuropean Grids Package

The Commission proposes faster grid-connection and storage permitting, and guidance that favours grid-friendly projects such as solar with storage. Source

1 Jan 2026

NLEnergiewet and revised ACM framework in force

The Energy Act replaces the Electricity Act and Gas Act, with new rules on grid connection. The revised framework keeps three priority categories: congestion relievers, safety, and basic needs. Source

29 May 2026

EUEPBD transposition deadline

Member states must bring the recast Energy Performance of Buildings Directive into national law. In July the Commission sent formal notices to all 27 member states for incomplete transposition. Source

1 Jul 2026

NLHousing construction enters priority category 3

New housing gains priority for grid capacity under basic needs, although priority is not a guaranteed connection. Source

Q4 2026

NLEnergiewet regulations under revision

Adjustments to regulations under the Energy Act are being prepared, and a congestion crisis act is announced in the coalition agreement 2026–2030. Source

31 Dec 2026

EUSolar mandate starts

The EPBD requires solar installations on new non-residential buildings from this date, and on all new residential buildings by 31 December 2029. Source

1 Jan 2027

NLCollective Heat Act expected

The act is expected to enter into force in phases, shifting how neighbourhood-scale energy systems are organised. Source

1 Jan 2028

EUZero-emission standard for new public buildings

First step of the EPBD zero-emission building requirement. Source

1 Jan 2030

EUAll new buildings zero-emission

Every new building in the EU must meet the standard, which raises the share of electricity in buildings. Source

The timeline is under construction and will grow to include policy beyond the Netherlands and the EU. Dates are as published by the sources linked.
